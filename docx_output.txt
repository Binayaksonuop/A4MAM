<p><strong>Nutrition:</strong></p><p>Nutrition is the process of providing the body with the essential materials required for life, growth, and health. It serves as the fuel for our physical and mental development. It is the foundation of <strong>growth, immunity and cognitive development</strong>. A child may receive enough food, yet still suffer from poor development if the diet lacks essential micronutrients. <br /><br />Nutrients are divided into four primary categories based on the body's needs:</p><ul><li><strong>Macronutrients:</strong> Required in <strong>large amounts</strong> to provide energy and building blocks for growth and maintenance. These include <strong>Carbohydrates</strong> (energy), <strong>Proteins</strong> (growth, repair, and immunity), and <strong>Fats</strong> (concentrated energy and brain development).</li><li><strong>Micronutrients:</strong> Required in <strong>small amounts</strong> but crucial for metabolism, immunity, and development. These include <strong>Vitamins</strong> (fat-soluble like A, D, E, K and water-soluble like B-complex and C) and <strong>Minerals</strong> (Iron, Iodine, Zinc, etc.).</li><li><strong>Water:</strong> Essential for hydration and nutrient transport.</li><li><strong>Dietary Fiber:</strong> Necessary for digestive health.</li></ul><p><strong>Malnutrition:</strong></p><p>Malnutrition occurs when the body does not receive <strong>adequate or balanced nutrients</strong> required for normal growth and health.<br />It affects both <strong>children and pregnant women</strong>, creating an intergenerational cycle.</p><p><strong>Hidden Hunger</strong></p><p>Even when calorie intake is sufficient, deficiency of micronutrients (iron, iodine, zinc, vitamin A, folate) leads to <strong>hidden hunger</strong> — common in cereal-based diets.</p><p><strong>Anemia</strong></p><p>One of the most visible outcomes of hidden hunger:</p><ul><li>Low hemoglobin → reduced oxygen carrying capacity</li><li>Mainly due to iron deficiency</li><li>Worsened by folate, B12 and vitamin A deficiency</li></ul><p>The findings from <strong>National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5)</strong> provided a clear picture of the nutritional status of children and women in India. The data highlighted that a significant proportion of children suffer from stunting, wasting and underweight, while anemia remains highly prevalent among both children and pregnant women. By analyzing this dataset, I developed a deeper understanding that malnutrition is not only the absence of food but the absence of a proper nutrition — including proteins, energy, vitamins and minerals. The survey also demonstrated that malnutrition exists at multiple levels: household food insecurity, poor maternal nutrition, inadequate infant feeding practices and micronutrient deficiency (hidden hunger). Through NFHS-5 indicators, I understood the importance of early-life nutrition, maternal health and supplementary feeding programs such as <strong>POSHAN Abhiyaan</strong> in breaking the intergenerational cycle of malnutrition. The data strengthened my understanding that improving awareness, dietary diversity and community-level interventions are equally important as food availability for achieving overall nutritional well-being.</p><p><br /><strong>Cycle</strong>: <br />Undernourished mother → Low birth weight baby → Malnourished child → Poor growth &amp; learning → Malnourished adult → Undernourished pregnancy<br /></p><p><strong>Critical Window: First 1000 Days (1000s)</strong></p><p>The period from pregnancy to a child’s second birthday is known as the <strong>"Window of Opportunity"</strong>. Stunting is defined as low height for a specific age, representing chronic, long-term malnutrition. For field assessments, stunting is considered a more reliable parameter because height is less prone to temporary manipulation or compromise compared to weight measurements.</p><p>Digital tools like the <strong>POSHAN app</strong> are employed to analyze graph data and charts that categorize children below 12 years of age into health ranges, such as "healthy" or malnourished.
